\noindent
\textbf{Setup:}
Autoware Universe runs on ROS~2 Humble on an Intel i7-12700H with six
performance and eight efficiency cores and an NVIDIA RTX~A1000. The input is
Autoware's Shinjuku map and sample rosbag, thirty seconds of recorded driving
replayed at half speed. Turbo and SMT are disabled so that frequency scaling and
thermal throttling do not enter the comparison, and a cgroup pins the
application to the six performance cores and the tracing daemons to six
efficiency cores, since the scheduler would otherwise migrate callbacks onto
efficiency cores and add noise. Each of the three modes, untraced, LTTng
(\texttt{ros2\_tracing}'s 14 events plus 28 FISH events plus
\texttt{sched\_switch}) and LTTng with \texttt{nsys}, is repeated three times,
and every reported value is taken from the steady-state phase of
Section~\ref{sec:method}. Out of the profiled session FISH generated an
application model with 88 executors, 241 nodes and 3451 callbacks linked by
6100 interactions on the callback level.

\noindent
\textbf{Overhead added with profiling:}
The untraced baseline carries no trace, so the only per-node values comparable
across the three modes are the processing times that Autoware's nodes measure
themselves, with a stopwatch inside their own callback, and report on
\texttt{/diagnostics}. Nineteen of the 241 nodes do: both GPU nodes, centerpoint
and the occupancy grid, and 17 CPU nodes, the fourteen crop-box,
distortion-corrector and ring-outlier filters of the three lidars, the
point-cloud concatenation, NDT scan matching and map-based prediction;
Table~\ref{tab:overhead-aw} averages each class. End-to-end latency grows by 1.7\,\%
under LTTng and 2.8\,\% with GPU profiling on top. In our experiments we find
that tracing changes how long the application takes, not what it does: the
perception pipeline keeps producing detected objects at the same rate
(9.9\,Hz in every mode), no centerpoint cycle is missed, and no topic state
monitor leaves \textsc{ok} in any of the nine runs, meaning the added latency
stays below what Autoware's own supervision treats as a fault. The GPU nodes
are the least affected, and one of them, the occupancy grid, even gets faster
(3.64 to 3.38\,ms): its callback ends in a blocking device-to-host copy that
waits behind the centerpoint inference sharing the GPU, so the time it reports
measures how the two callbacks collide on the accelerator, which tracing
perturbs, rather than the cost of observing it.
